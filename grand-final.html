THE MOST CONTROVERSIAL
GRAND FINAL YET

Ligma League Investigations Unit · Grand Final Week · 3rd Edition

Every league has a Grand Final. Very few have a Grand Final where one of the participants owns a piece of the other one. That is the situation the Ligma League has walked itself into, eyes wide open, paperwork unread, and it is now too late to do anything about it.

To understand how we arrived here, you have to go back to before a single ball was bounced this season. Before the draft. Before the trades. Before Max Hall became the most argued about name in the competition. You have to go back to one fateful afternoon at the Australian Open.

The details of that afternoon remain, by every available account, incomplete. What is known is this. The Better Holmes and Gardens were at a genuine crossroads. Years of frustration had piled up. Multiple bottom four finishes had done real damage. There were serious conversations, some of them public, most of them not, about walking away from the league entirely. A foundation club was one bad conversation away from being a former club.

Meanwhile, on the other side of the same afternoon, the Van Twists had comfortably worked their way through what witnesses have described as an inadvisable number of Balter's, and apparently identified what they believed to be a market inefficiency. Where everyone else saw a club in decline, the Van Twists saw an investment opportunity.

The deal that followed has never been fully explained to anyone. The buy in was covered. The club was saved. And in return, the Van Twists walked away with a 25% stake in Better Holmes and Gardens' winnings.

Nobody blinked at the time. There was no announcement, no ratification, no governance process, because the Ligma League has never had any of those things. It was a handshake in the sun, allegedly at a tennis tournament, allegedly under the influence, and it is now the single most financially significant document in league history.

HOLMES
The Favourite

BETTER HOLMES
AND GARDENS

The team that has had everything to lose all year.

Here is the part of the story that nobody disputes. Since round one, Better Holmes and Gardens have been the best team in the Ligma League. Not the luckiest. Not the loudest. The best.

That sentence would have been a punchline twelve months ago. This is a club that made a habit of the bottom four, that turned late season irrelevance into an art form, and that spent more time managing disappointment than managing a forward line. The team that was seriously considering leaving the league has instead spent the entire season at the top of it.

The pressure has been constant, because favouritism in this competition is a burden rather than a privilege. Every week there has been a target on their back. Every week they have handled it. They have gone from potential league exit to genuine premiership favourite in a single season.

And then there is the irony, which is almost too neat. To finish the job, they have to beat the person who kept them in the league. Their minority shareholder is sitting on the other side of the matchup, wearing the opposition colours, holding 25% of the winnings either way.

They have dominated all year. Now they have to finish the job.

The Contender

THE VAN TWISTS

From league controversy to Grand Final contender.

For most of the season, the Van Twists were nowhere near this. Not in the conversation, not in the frame, not remotely a threat to anybody's premiership plans. They were a team with an investment portfolio and not much else.

Then came the night that changed everything.

During a chaotic waiver period, the Van Twists glitched the season and somehow emerged holding Max Hall. The league erupted immediately. The questions started within minutes and, months later, they still have not been answered.

Was this a glitch?
Was this legal?
Was this cheating?
Was the system simply exploited?
Should Max Hall have even been available?
Did the Van Twists know exactly what they were doing?

Regardless of the answer, the league was furious. Allegations flew. Screenshots were circulated. Integrity, a word previously unknown to this competition, was suddenly being used in full sentences.

And then Max Hall started delivering. Week after week, the player who caused the controversy became the player dragging the Van Twists towards the promised land. The scandal became a weapon. The team that came from nowhere is now ninety minutes from a premiership.

The Defining Controversy

THE MAX HALLAFFAIR

Before
The season was heading one way.

The ladder made sense. The favourites were the favourites. The Van Twists were a mid table curiosity with a strange financial interest in someone else's success.

The Night
Then, one night, everything changed.

The Van Twists somehow navigated the waiver system and emerged with Max Hall. How it happened depends entirely on who you ask, and nobody agrees with anybody.

The Fallout
The league immediately went into meltdown.

Rumours spread. Managers questioned the integrity of the waiver system for the first time in three editions. Old grudges were reopened for no reason other than the atmosphere.

The Verdicts
Some called it a glitch. Some called it exploitation. Some called it cheating.

The Van Twists called it winning.

The Consequence
And Max Hall went on to become one of the central reasons they are now playing in the Grand Final.

The player at the centre of the storm turned into the engine room of a premiership campaign. Whatever the league believes about how he arrived, he has been worth every ounce of the argument.

The league wanted an investigation.
The Van Twists wanted a premiership.
Max Hall wanted points.
